2Finite State Machine
Q2.1Finite State Machines - Filament replacement (2pts.)
Next, you will model some basic high-level control operations of your
3D printer. Namely, you will model the replacement of filament in
these two situations:
• When a new colour is required to be printed(C), or
• When the currently being used spool is finished(F).
We start modelling these two distinct situations as being triggered
by two, independent, discrete signalsCandF. Once either of these
events is detected, the following set of steps needs to be taken:
1. Printing stops, saving the current location of the print head.
2. Moving to a wipe-tower location(x p,y p), which we assume to be
at(0, 0).
3. Excess filament is wiped (by printing on the wiping location).
4. The nozzle heat is switched off.
5. New filament is loaded.
6. The nozzle heat is switched on.
7. Printing is resumed.
Model the operation first as a Finite State Machine (FSM) of the3D
printer, including a state for “printing" and any necessary states to
cover the filament replacement process.Start by modelling the process with
an arbitrary wipe location first.
Q2.2Automata - Parked detection (3pts.)
Your colleague forgot to order end-of-line sensors, but thankfully, the
motors include rotational sensors you can use as a virtual end-of-line
sensor. Simply put, you can detect an end-of-line by testing whether
or not the motors are rotating to produce a vertical or horizontal
motion when the corresponding voltages are applied to them. Let
us assume the printer head is always brought to the origin via the
concatenation of two types of motions:
• Vertical (V) until we hit the bottom of the printing area,
• Horizontal (H) until we hit the left-most side of the printing area.
